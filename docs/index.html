Abstract

SAM 2 segments and tracks an object across a video in 2D: click once, get a mask in every frame. Those masks are 2D. To get a 3D mask you must lift them onto a 3D representation of the scene. Lifting with Euclidean k-nearest-neighbors makes the label jump across gaps, a hand in front of a mug, two walls at a concave corner, anywhere points are close in space but far along the surface, so the mask fragments. GeoSAM-3D reconstructs the scene as 3D Gaussians, lifts SAM 2 masks onto them, and propagates the label along geodesic distance (distance along the surface manifold) using the heat method. Two points across an occlusion are geodesically far even when spatially close, so the label stops at the real boundary. Only a small per-Gaussian feature head is trained; the large models stay frozen.

Honest scope. The novel, working, tested piece is the heat-method geodesic propagation kernel (propagate/heat_geodesic.py, 4 property tests) plus the per-Gaussian contrastive feature head. The two heavy front-end pieces, MonoGS reconstruction and SAM 2 mask projection, are off-the-shelf models wired in and are currently stubbed in this repo (the runner returns empty tensors; mask projection returns random ids). The ScanNet / Replica / ScanNet++ leaderboard numbers are a target, not a reproduced result. The 15 unit/smoke tests (verified passing) validate the kernel and the pipeline plumbing, not a trained segmentation score.

How it works

Freeze the big models, train a tiny head. The contribution is the geodesic propagation on the Gaussian graph.

The heat method, in one paragraph

Varadhan's classical result: drop a unit of heat at a seed and diffuse it for a tiny time t; the heat that reaches a point x decays like exp(-d²/4t), where d is the geodesic distance. Invert it: d ≈ √(-4t·log u). GeoSAM-3D solves one implicit heat step (I + tL)u = u₀ on the kNN-graph Laplacian, then reads geodesic distance off the diffused heat and turns it into a per-Gaussian label probability. It deliberately skips Crane et al.'s gradient-normalize + Poisson stage, which degenerates at seed nodes on a non-mesh kNN graph; the one-step Varadhan form is monotone in u and sufficient for ordering points. The geodesic machinery is the same manifold-distance primitive as the author's thesis work (TGARD for trajectory-gap detection), a deliberate cross-project through-line.

Why it matters for street-level 3D

Promptable 3D from one camera

Click the curb, the step, the doorway in a single delivery clip and get its 3D mask without leaking onto the road behind it. That is exactly the street-level, single-moving-camera capture setting for last-mile maps and safety.

An owned primitive, not a one-off

The geodesic kernel is the same manifold-distance idea the author's dissertation uses for trajectory anomalies (TGARD). It is a reused, tested primitive that ports across 3D vision and spatiotemporal anomaly detection.
